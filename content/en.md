Enterprise RSI needs an optimization space that will not be exhausted anytime soon. This space may come from scientific exploration or competitive games (safety, trading). Many people believe RSI should involve pretraining. Others believe RSI may also be possible at the harness layer, as long as its improvements feed back into a greater ability to make improvements in the next round. Before discussing enterprise RSI, let us first discuss enterprise tasks. **I roughly divide tasks into two categories—or two ends of a spectrum.**

## Type 1: Mapped-Space Tasks

These mainly include five categories:

1. Customer Support
2. Legal & Compliance
3. HR & Recruiting
4. Business Operations
5. Financial Operations

These tasks share two characteristics: their optimization ceilings are relatively clear, and changes mainly come from local updates, such as new product releases, changes in the law, or adjustments to recruiting processes. Many scenarios do not need RSI; they just need an FDE to build the workflow well.

## Type 2: Open Ended Problems

These tasks require a defined goal, tools, an environment, constraints, evaluation criteria, and a search space: What can the system change or discover? For example, when improving training efficiency, the search space needs to be clear: Can the system modify the learning rate, optimizer, model architecture, CUDA kernels, training pipeline, and so on?

### Example 1: Improving Model Training Efficiency

**Goal:** Reduce training cost while maintaining model quality.

**Tools:** Python, PyTorch, CUDA, profiling tools.

**Environment:** Training infrastructure and GPU clusters.

**Constraints:** GPU budget, memory, runtime.

**Evaluation:** Validation loss, throughput, training cost, reproducibility.

**Search Space:** Optimizers, model architectures, kernels, training algorithms.

### Example 2: Scientific Discovery RSI

**Goal:** Identify and validate disease-relevant therapeutic targets.

**Tools:** Bioinformatics, literature search, molecular modeling.

**Environment:** Public databases, computational and laboratory experiments.

**Constraints:** Biological plausibility, safety, experimental cost.

**Evaluation:** Experimental validation, reproducibility, causal evidence.

**Search Space:** Genes, proteins, pathways, mechanisms.

A characteristic of this field is that evaluation is expensive and time-consuming.

### Example 3: GTM RSI

**Goal:** Maximize incremental downloads and revenue.

**Tools:** Social media, advertising platforms, analytics.

**Environment:** Real-world market.

**Constraints:** Budget, brand, platform policies.

**Evaluation:** Incremental conversion, CAC, retention, LTV.

**Search Space:** Content, channels, audiences, pricing, campaigns.

## Two Mechanisms That Continuously Expand the Frontier

RSI with lasting value needs an optimization space that continues to expand.

This space may come from scientific exploration or competitive games.

### Mechanism 1: Exploration-driven Frontier

Continuously discovering new possibilities through exploration.

**1. Life Science**

Humans can continue to explore new disease mechanisms, drug targets, treatments, and biological mechanisms.

**2. Entertainment**

Human demand for entertainment experiences keeps changing.

Even with a vast number of existing games, new gameplay, interactions, and forms of entertainment can still emerge.

### Mechanism 2: Competition-driven Frontier

Continuously raising capability requirements through competition.

**1. Finance**

A trading strategy that works today may stop working tomorrow because competitors adopt similar strategies.

**2. Cybersecurity**

As attackers become more capable, defense systems need to keep improving.

**3. GTM**

An ad creative that works today may lose its effectiveness when competitors adopt better content, platform algorithms change, or consumer interests shift.

Information decays at different speeds across domains.

| Domain | Information Decay | Need for Continuous Optimization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entertainment | User preferences keep changing | Continuously explore new experiences |
| Finance | Market information decays rapidly | Continuously discover new strategies |
| Cybersecurity | Offensive and defensive capabilities keep changing | Constantly adapt to adversaries |
| GTM | Ad performance, user preferences, and platform algorithms change | Continuously optimize campaign strategies |

Financial markets are not only competitive; information also decays quickly. This makes them particularly well suited to automated research and continuous optimization.

## Five Directions Worth Exploring for RSI

1. Life Science
2. Entertainment
3. Finance
4. Cybersecurity
5. GTM

These fields have expanding optimization spaces, primarily driven by two mechanisms:

- **Exploration-driven Frontier:** Discovering new possibilities through exploration.
- **Competition-driven Frontier:** Raising capability requirements through competition.

## V. Robotics: An Important Bridge for RSI into Traditional Physical Industries

Why do potential directions for RSI seem concentrated in a few digital fields? Traditional manufacturing, agriculture, construction, and other industries seem insufficiently covered. One important reason is that AI can currently operate directly in digital environments, but cannot directly perform many physical tasks. Robotics is an important bridge for RSI into traditional physical industries. Many traditional industries currently cannot complete an entire optimization loop with software agents alone. Robotics can extend RSI from digital space into physical space.

## VI. RSI Needs a Third Condition: Economic Sustainability

Can continuous improvements in capability create enough economic value to support the next round of optimization?

Even if continuous optimization is technically possible, sustaining long-term spending on compute is difficult if the improvements cannot generate sufficient commercial value.

RSI therefore needs not only an expanding optimization space and recursive improvement, but also consideration of economic sustainability.

**The core question: Can continuous improvements in capability create enough economic value to support the next round of optimization?**

For example, in my view, Anthropic’s research on [designing proteins that bind to scorpion neurotoxins](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-scorpion-toxin-binder-design) is probably not a study that can generate much economic value.
